Revenue

Sales
September revenue of R91,875 was R52,460 (36%) below August, but August included the once-off R73,056 Wellness Warehouse trade order. Stripping that out, underlying revenue grew about 29% month on month and was 15% ahead of July. The direct online channel recovered strongly: Payfast (Shopify checkout) more than doubled to R22,373, its best month since April, and Amazon reached a new high of R12,422. Takealot eased 17% to R35,679, its lowest month since December, and resellers contributed R18,917, of which R10,039 is a new Premed order. GP margin recovered to 57.5% from 52.7% as the mix moved back towards full-price online sales, although COGS courier and fulfilment rose to R10,269 from R1,646 and held the margin back. That increase relates to moving purchased stock rather than to customer deliveries. The month closed with a net loss of R16,976, the first loss since February, driven by the lower reseller volume and a R9,898 rise in operating expenses (social media advertising up R7,191 to R9,804, courier and delivery up R3,571, Amazon costs up R1,830).

Margin per Box

Unit economics
Sold at full price, every box earns a product margin of 75–83%. What it keeps after channel costs depends mainly on where it is sold: about 70% of the selling price on Amazon, 58–63% through resellers and around 53% on 30-packs through Takealot and Shopify. 15-packs keep less on Takealot and Shopify because fees and courier costs are much the same per order whatever the pack size, so encouraging 30-pack sales on those channels lifts the margin without changing prices.

Delivery Footprint

Courier Guy
This section maps every Courier Guy delivery billed in Xero from March to September 2026: 319 deliveries, covering direct online and Yoco orders (Takealot and Amazon fulfil their own deliveries and are not included). Two regions account for almost three quarters of all deliveries: the Western Cape at 39% (Cape Town alone 31%) and Gauteng at 33% (Johannesburg 19%, Pretoria 11%). KwaZulu-Natal adds 11%, the remaining six provinces together make up 16%, and 1% are unallocated. Monthly delivery volume averaged 68 from March to May, then fell to 22 a month from June to August. September recovered to 47, mostly in Cape Town (21) and Johannesburg (12), tracking the doubling of Payfast sales and the step-up in social media advertising.

What this means for the business. First, marketing can be targeted: with 72% of deliveries going to the Western Cape and Gauteng, paid social spend can be weighted to those two regions rather than spread nationally, and the September result suggests the ad spend is converting. Second, the mid-year drop shows the direct customer base was not retained through winter; a reorder or subscription offer to past customers in these two regions is likely to be the cheapest growth available. Third, Gauteng is a large market for direct orders, all delivered by courier; a Gauteng-based reseller or pharmacy partner could cut delivery cost and time for about a third of deliveries.

Sales

Resellers contributed R79,211, of which R73,056 was a single trade order from Wellness Warehouse for 240 x 30-packs and 160 x 15-packs at a discounted trade price of roughly 52% of retail.
